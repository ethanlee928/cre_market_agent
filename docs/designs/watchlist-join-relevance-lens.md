## Problem Statement

`docs/handoffs/watchlist-join.md` calls the watchlist join "the product's core lever":
attaching a market fact to a specific building the user owns. Today the join matches on
submarket and grade only. No detector reads `lease_expiry`, so the brief says "you hold
two of these" and stops.

Reading the handoff against the running code found four things that change the work.

**F1. The north-star sentence cannot be built.** It ends "Your passing rent of £82 psf
sits above the £71 Grade B average." There is no £71:

```
grade_b_rent_avg | West End | 2026H1 | value: None | yoy: -11.3%
                 | note: "Level not stated in source"
```

Savills published the change and withheld the level — seed defect E-4, the one `store.py`
exists to survive. The only Grade B level in the dataset is City at £45.66. So the
handoff's acceptance criterion 1 contradicts its own guardrail three sections above it
("every figure comes from a tool call, never generated").

**F2. Work item 4 wires the narrative contract into the wrong layer.** Criteria 1 and 3
want the decision verb on the front page before the user types. The front page renders
`Signal.detail`, a deterministic f-string. Work item 4 says wire the contract into
`SYSTEM_PROMPT` in `gemini.py`, which only reaches chat. On the keyless path — a README
headline feature — there is no model, so no decision verb renders anywhere.

**F3. `affected` currently matches everything.** `supply_shock` calls
`_match(watchlist, submarket=None)`, which filters on nothing and returns every asset.
Exact current output:

```
[RISK       ] City Grade B rents falling while Grade A rises (-2.6% against +7.0%)
              affected=['Clerkenwell Works']
[RISK       ] West End Grade B rents falling while Grade A rises (-11.3% against +4.0%)
              affected=['Mayfair House']
[WATCH      ] Record 7,700,000 sq ft of completions in 2026, only 35% pre-let
              affected=['120 Fenchurch Street','Mayfair House','Clerkenwell Works']
[OPPORTUNITY] 41 large requirements chasing 21 options (2.0:1)
              affected=['120 Fenchurch Street']
```

`sort_key` ranks "has affected assets" first. Verified, the spurious match buys
`supply_shock` exactly one position, above the OPPORTUNITY signal: with `affected=[]` it
sorts to `(1,1)` against `large_occupier_squeeze` at `(0,2)`. Both RISK signals stay above
it either way. The defect is that the page asserts an exposure the system cannot justify,
not that the ordering collapses.

**F4, the useful accident.** `covers('City Fringe','City')` is `True`, so Clerkenwell
Works (Grade B, City Fringe) already matches the *City* `quality_spread`, where both
levels are published (£76.21 Grade A, £45.66 Grade B). Mayfair House matches the West End
one, where the Grade B level is not. That asymmetry is the design.

## What Makes This Cool

**The two evidence classes, defined once.** Class 1 is *published aggregate*: grade
averages from the Savills report, plus arithmetic over them and the user's own config.
Class 2 is *named comparable*: a specific signed letting with an achieved rent. Class 2 is
only admissible when class 1 exists for that asset, because without a published grade
average you cannot say whether a named deal is a ceiling or a like-for-like.

Two buildings, the same ladder, one missing rung. Adjacent on the front page.

**Clerkenwell Works** — City Fringe, Grade B, 38,000 sq ft — gets every rung:

| Rung | Value | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| What you're paid | £52.00 psf passing | user config (Tier 3) |
| What the grade averages | £45.66 psf Grade B, City, 2026H1 | Savills |
| What the grade above averages | £76.21 psf Grade A, City, 2026H1 | Savills |
| What actually signed nearby | **Fractile AI, JJ Mack Building EC1, 21,734 sq ft at £115.00 psf, Q2 2026** | Savills, named deal |

Two numbers fall out, each answering a different decision:

- **Reversion risk:** (52.00 − 45.66) × 38,000 = **£240,920/yr** of income above market,
  exposed at the next review. That quantifies *regear*.
- **Grade-gap upside:** (76.21 − 45.66) × 38,000 = **£1,160,900/yr** gross, before capex
  and voids. That quantifies *refurbish*.
- **Uplift over what you actually earn:** (76.21 − 52.00) × 38,000 = **£919,980/yr**.

**The first two share a baseline and must never be summed.** £240,920 + £919,980 =
£1,160,900 exactly, because the grade gap is measured from the Grade B average while the
building already earns £6.34 above it. Render all three, or render the reversion risk and
the £919,980 uplift and drop the £1.16m. A CRE reader will spot an additive presentation
in the demo.

**Mayfair House** — Mayfair, Grade B, 24,000 sq ft — is missing rung 2, and that
cascades. West End Grade B has no published level, so there is no benchmark, so no named
deal can be classified as a ceiling rather than a comp, so **the comparable rung is
suppressed too**. What it gets instead: passing £82 psf, West End Grade B down 11.3% YoY,
Grade A at £106.17, a break in 2027-09 inside the 2026H2–2029 pipeline window, and an
explicit line saying Savills does not publish the level.

That suppression rule is not a workaround, it is the thesis. The seed holds two West End
lettings with achieved rents — Strategic Value Partners at £182.50 and Softbank at
£201.00 — and both would attach to Mayfair House under a naive submarket filter.
Hanging a £201 psf prime Mayfair deal off a Grade B secondary building is precisely the
"caught out" failure this product exists to prevent. The system declines because it
cannot show its working.

The contrast is the demo: a product pitching auditability, proving on its own front page
that it knows which rung it is missing and what that costs it.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: inline match, reason strings per detector — rejected
Extend `matching()`, return `Match` objects, each detector builds its reason inline.
Smallest diff, lowest merge surface, but copies the matching logic across four detectors
and makes P3's fix a thing to remember four times rather than a property of the design.

### Approach B: relevance lens with declared signal scope
Detectors stop matching. Each `Signal` carries a `Scope`; one module walks every signal
against the watchlist and attaches `Match` objects.

### Approach C: B plus an asset-first front page — rejected
Invert the layout so each building leads with its own row. Best screenshot in the project,
but a layout rebuild on Saturday night needing a per-asset ranking rule that brushes
against P4.

### Approach B-plus: B, plus the named comparable ladder — **chosen**

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B-plus**, chosen by the user (D10).

An empty `Scope()` matches nothing, which is the correct default, so F3 is fixed as a
property rather than as a thing to remember in four places. The whole join lands under one
test file, which is criterion 7 and its named boundary cases. The ladder is the memorable
half, and it reads the 17 seed events, which today reach the `find_market_activity` chat
tool but no detector and no front page.

### Shape

`Match` lives in `src/cre_agent/watchlist.py`, so the import direction stays
`signals → watchlist` and never reverses. `Scope` lives in the new
`src/cre_agent/lens.py`, because `Scope.rent_benchmark` is a `store.Fact` and
`watchlist.py` must not start importing `store`.

```python
# watchlist.py
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Match:
    asset: str        # asset name
    reason: str       # why this signal touches this building, with figures
    action: str       # decision verb from the controlled vocabulary

# lens.py
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Scope:
    submarket: str | None = None
    grade: str | None = None
    min_sqft: int | None = None
    lease_event_between: tuple[str, str] | None = None   # ("2026-07", "2029-12")
    rent_benchmark: Fact | None = None
    comparable: dict | None = None    # a store.events letting, or None to suppress
```

`Signal` gains `scope: Scope = Scope()` and `affected` becomes `list[Match]`. Detectors
set `scope` and stop calling `_match`. `detect_all` collects signals, then calls
`lens.apply(signals, watchlist)` before sorting.

**Import graph, and the cycle to avoid.** `signals.py` imports `Scope` from `lens.py`;
`lens.py` must therefore import `Signal` only under `if TYPE_CHECKING:`. Both modules
already carry `from __future__ import annotations`, so the annotations are strings and
this works. A module-level `from .signals import Signal` in `lens.py` is an `ImportError`.

**`matching()` after migration** returns objects, not names, because the lens needs
`Asset` fields to build reasons:

```python
def matching(self, submarket=None, grade=None, min_sqft=None) -> list[Asset]:
```

Lease-window and comparable filtering stay in `lens.py`; `watchlist.py` never imports
`store`.

**Resolving an asset to its grade benchmark.** Store facts exist only for `"City"` and
`"West End"`; assets sit in `"City Fringe"`, `"Mayfair"`, `"City Core"`. There is no
reverse lookup, so walk the hierarchy upward and take the first published hit:

```python
def benchmark(store, index, asset, metric):
    for sid in index.ancestors(index.resolve(asset.submarket) or ""):
        f = store.get(metric, index.by_id[sid].label)
        if f is not None:
            return f
    return None
```

**The E-4 trap in the type signature.** `rent_benchmark: Fact | None` is truthy for the
West End Grade B fact, whose `value` is `None`. A bare `if scope.rent_benchmark:` would
un-suppress Mayfair House's comparable and attach the £201 Softbank deal — the repo's own
named defect walking back in through a type. The test is always:

```python
scope.rent_benchmark is not None and scope.rent_benchmark.value is not None
```

**`comparable` belongs on `Match`, not `Scope`.** One signal can match several assets with
different comparables. Resolve it per matched asset inside `lens.apply` and store it on
the `Match`. A per-signal comparable happens not to bite on the shipped three-asset
config, which is exactly how it would survive into production.

### Choosing `Match.action`

| Signal | Condition | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| `quality_spread` | passing rent above the grade benchmark | `regear` |
| `quality_spread` | grade gap present, EPC at or above C | `refurbish` |
| `quality_spread` | grade gap present, EPC below C | `defer capex` |
| `supply_shock` | break inside the pipeline window | `start the conversation` |
| `submarket_divergence` | vacancy above benchmark | `re-price` |
| any | no condition fires | `hold`, with the reason stated |

`hold` is the default and is a real answer, not a fallthrough. `monitor` is not in the
tuple and is asserted absent.

**Lease-window comparison is `(year, month)` tuples parsed from `YYYY-MM` strings, in
`lens.py`.** Do not route this through `Period`. Verified: `Period.parse("2027-09")`
raises `SeedSchemaError`, and `Period.parse("2026H2-2029")` discards the `H2` — `.months`
returns `(1, 12)` — so `contains(Period("2026Q1"))` returns `True` for a window that opens
in July. The handoff's Work item 2 already prescribes tuple comparison. The window bounds
are derived from `pipeline_to_2029`'s `Period.raw`, not from the parsed object.

`Asset` gains three optional fields: `break_date`, `passing_rent_psf`, `epc_rating`.
(`tenant` is dropped — nothing in this plan consumes it.) The empty-watchlist path stays
fully functional. Literal values for the shipped config:

| Asset | `break_date` | `passing_rent_psf` | `epc_rating` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 120 Fenchurch Street | *(none — falls back to expiry 2031-06)* | 68.00 | B |
| Mayfair House | 2027-09 | 82.00 | D |
| Clerkenwell Works | 2028-03 | 52.00 | C |

120 Fenchurch Street deliberately has no break date: it exercises the expiry fallback and
is correctly excluded from the `("2026-07", "2029-12")` window.

### Decision-verb vocabulary

One tuple in `lens.py`, asserted by test: `regear`, `refurbish`, `re-price`, `hold`,
`defer capex`, `start the conversation`. The `SYSTEM_PROMPT` addition in `gemini.py`
keeps **both** halves of the handoff's contract: never close with "monitor", and do not
restate a precomputed per-asset figure differently.

### Macro — the gilt spread only (D11: C)

Resolved against the sibling appendix's item 9 rather than the handoff's three-number
scope. **One number: the UK 10-year gilt yield, fetched once, cached to disk with a
timestamp, rendered from cache.** The demo never depends on a live call and criterion 5
survives.

Probe first, for 20 minutes: the BoE series `IUDBEDR` is reportedly a keyless CSV over
stdlib `urllib.request`. If it holds, the figure is reproducible rather than live, and it
is a *stronger* evidence class than a grounded search parse — so it must not render as
`grounded`. Name the endpoint and the retrieval date explicitly rather than collapsing it
into the search-grounded bucket. If the probe fails, one grounded lookup, cached.

The payoff is the reason this number and not another: `prime_yield` is already in the seed
for City (5.25%) and West End (3.75%). Gilt subtracted from prime yield is the risk
premium, and that is what explains investment turnover sitting 35% below its 10-year
average. One number, one derived spread, one sentence.
