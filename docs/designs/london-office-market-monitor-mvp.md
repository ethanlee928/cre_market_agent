# Design: London Office Market Monitor (MVP)

## Problem Statement

A CRE business team needs to stay ahead of shifts in the London office market. Today
they read quarterly PDFs from Savills, CBRE and JLL, and do ad-hoc web research when
someone asks a question. That is slow, it is not monitored between quarters, and the
figures they repeat in client meetings are hard to source under challenge.

The end user is non-technical: a portfolio manager or agency lead, not an analyst and
not an engineer. Deliverable is a weekend proof of concept plus 3 to 6 slides, as a
technical test for a Forward Deployed Engineer role.

Four jobs the user actually has, in priority order:

1. Do not let me get caught out. Being surprised in a client meeting is a career risk.
2. Give me a defensible number in ten seconds, with its source and date.
3. Tell me what it means for us, not for the market in the abstract.
4. Give me a narrative I can use, not a dashboard I have to interpret.

## What Makes This Cool

The brief lands before you type anything. You open a tab and three ranked signals are
already there, each one filtered to the buildings you actually own. Then you press
Refresh and watch the agent's tool calls stream past on screen: reading market_data,
searching news, checking your watchlist. That moment is the proof it is an agent and
not a chat wrapper, and it is the thing worth demoing live.

The underlying market data is genuinely interesting right now, which makes the demo
carry itself. Three forces pulling against each other:

- Demand at a record 15.7m sq ft active, +69% on the long-run average, AI-driven.
  Anthropic took 158,138 sq ft at 1 Triton Square; OpenAI took 77,521 sq ft at York Way.
  The West End outsold the City on the QUARTER for the first time since 2021. (Corrected
  in /autoplan Phase 3: an earlier draft dropped "on the quarter" and read as an H1 claim,
  which the seed contradicts. H1 take-up was West End 2.2m against City 2.6m. This is
  exactly the "caught out" failure the product exists to prevent, so narrative claims must
  be asserted against the store in tests.)
- Supply about to spike. 2026 completions heading for a record 7.7m sq ft, +43% on the
  10-year average, only 35% pre-let. Meanwhile development starts collapsed to 1.5m sq ft
  with a single new-build.
- Investment lagging badly. H1 turnover 35% below the 10-year average, and 36% of Q2's
  total was one Barclays purchase.

Plus a clean flight-to-quality divergence: City Grade A rents +7% YoY while Grade B fell
2.6%, and West End Grade B fell 11.3%. That is exactly the kind of thing the agent should
surface unprompted.

## Constraints

- Two days. Friday evening 2026-08-28 through Sunday 2026-08-30 midnight.
- Gemini 3.7 Flash via google-genai, key supplied through GOOGLE_API_KEY so a grader can
  swap in their own. Confirmed capabilities: 1M input context, function calling, Google
  Search grounding, structured outputs, URL context, thinking levels low/medium/high.
- Data is real, not synthetic. Savills Central London Office Market Watch Q2 2026,
  published 2026-08-06, harvested in full to data/seed_2026Q2.json: 47 facts, 17 named
  events, 6 sector rows. Attribution is mandatory in the app and in the slides.
- savills.co.uk returns HTTP 403 to generic HTTP clients. Google's crawler is allowed.
  This is why retrieval goes through Gemini Search grounding and URL context rather than
  httpx, and why the historical harvest is a build-time browser job, not a runtime path.
- Must run for a grader with: an API key, one install command, one run command.

## Premises

All five confirmed with the user before design.

- P1. The brief is the product, the chat is the follow-up. Value arrives before the user
  types anything. A blank chat box shifts the work onto someone who does not know what
  to ask.
- P2. Relevance beats coverage. One signal filtered to their two Grade B West End assets
  beats a complete summary of all seven key areas. This is the argument for the watchlist.
  Market-wide must still work perfectly with an empty watchlist, so the brief's literal
  ask is satisfied and the watchlist is additive rather than a substitution.
- P3. Every number carries its source and as-of date. An unsourced figure is a liability,
  not just useless. The agent visibly says "I do not have that" rather than guessing.
- P4 (AMENDED, /autoplan Phase 1). Two quarters minimum, plus signal-change tracking.
  The original premise said one quarter was enough because Savills publishes its own
  QoQ and YoY comparisons. That is true for reporting a number and false for monitoring
  one: with a single static quarter every detector emits identical output on every run,
  so the brief says the same thing on Sunday that it said on Friday. Harvest Q1 2026 as
  a second quarter so deltas are computed rather than quoted, and persist signal state
  between runs so the brief can lead with what changed. Beyond two quarters, extra
  history buys chart shape only and stays first on the cut list.
- P5. Setup is part of the deliverable. More than key plus install plus run, and the
  non-technical user claim collapses on contact.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Precomputed brief + chat
Page renders entirely from seed JSON; the agent powers only the chat box. Effort S, risk
low. Rejected: ships fastest but invites "where is the agent?" with only a chat box as
the answer, and the skills architecture ends up exercised only by chat.

### Approach B: Agent generates everything
Every brief render is a full agent run; skills are the only code path. Effort M, risk
high. Rejected: 30 to 60 seconds of blank screen per load, non-reproducible output that
cannot be screenshotted for slides with confidence, and total dependency on the API
staying up during a live demo.

### Approach C: Deterministic spine, agentic edges
Metrics and detectors are plain Python: fast, testable, identical every run. The agent
owns interpretation, news and Q&A. The brief renders instantly from cache; a visible
Refresh button runs the agent and streams its tool calls into the UI.

## Recommended Approach

Approach C, chosen by the user.

The judgement an FDE is hired for is knowing where the model earns its keep and where it
does not. C makes that judgement visible. It is also the only one of the three that
demos reliably live, and the only one whose numbers you can defend in the room because
they come out of unit-tested Python rather than a prompt.

### Where the line falls

Deterministic Python:
- Fact store and query layer over the seed data
- The four signal detectors and their severity ranking
- Watchlist loading and relevance matching
- Every figure rendered on the brief page

Agent (Gemini 3.7 Flash):
- Interpretation and narrative for each signal
- News scan via Google Search grounding
- All chat, with tool access to the store, detectors and watchlist
- Macro context pulled live

### Skills

Each is a SKILL.md plus tools, loaded on demand, and each maps to a key area from the
brief so coverage is visible.

| Skill | Job |
|---|---|
| market_data | Query rents, vacancy, take-up, availability by submarket and grade |
| supply_pipeline | Completions, under construction, pre-let share, development starts |
| macro_context | Live BoE Bank Rate and gilts, ONS employment and inflation |
| news_scan | Google Search grounding over CRE sources, classified and entity-extracted |
| watchlist_lens | Match a market fact to the user's own assets and exposures |

### Detectors

Four, not eight. Each returns severity, headline, evidence list and affected assets.

- quality_spread: Grade A versus Grade B divergence by submarket (currently +7% against
  -2.6% in the City, and -11.3% in the West End)
- supply_shock: completions against pre-let share against absorption
- submarket_divergence: a submarket's vacancy against the Central London average
  (City Core 5.9%, City Fringe 8.0%, Hammersmith 22%)
- large_occupier_squeeze: 21 available options over 100k sq ft against 41 live
  requirements, with only 5 of those options in Core submarkets

## Success Criteria

- A non-technical person opens the app and understands the market in 30 seconds without
  typing anything.
- Every figure on screen carries a source and an as-of date.
- Chat answers "should we be worried about the City Fringe?" with real numbers and
  citations, and declines to answer what it does not know.
- Pressing Refresh visibly streams the agent's tool calls.
- With an empty watchlist the market-wide view is still complete and useful.
- A grader gets from clone to running app with a key, one install command and one run
  command.

## Distribution Plan

Local run, no hosting. README documents: copy .env.example to .env and add
GOOGLE_API_KEY, then `uv sync`, then `streamlit run app.py`. A --demo flag backed by
cached fixtures runs the whole app with no key at all, so a grader without an AI Studio
account still sees it work end to end. No CI, no deploy pipeline; out of scope for a
weekend proof of concept and stated as such on the slides.

## Next Steps

Ordered. Each line is buildable and testable on its own.

1. Fact store and query layer. Load seed JSON, query(metric, submarket, period),
   compare(). Pure Python, unit tested. This is the spine.
2. Watchlist. watchlist.yaml plus loader, with three sample assets carrying submarket,
   grade, sq ft and lease expiry.
3. Detectors. The four above, each returning a Signal with evidence and affected assets.
   Unit tested against the seed data.
4. LLM adapter. google-genai client, tool declarations, manual function-calling loop
   (not automatic, so tool calls can be streamed to the UI), provider-swappable interface.
5. Skills. The five above, as SKILL.md plus tool functions.
6. Streamlit app. Brief page first, then chat, then the Refresh button with streamed
   tool calls.
7. Demo fixtures for keyless running.
8. Charts. First thing to cut if time runs short.
9. Slides. Five: the problem and the job, how it works, what you get, trust and
   provenance, limits and what production looks like.

### Scope expansions approved in /autoplan Phase 1 (SELECTIVE EXPANSION)

- E1. Harvest Q1 2026 as a second quarter, via the browser path already proven on Q2.
  Moves into step 1, ahead of the store, because the store's schema should be validated

- E2. An "as of" staleness banner carrying the publication date of the underlying data,

The gate initially failed with `400 INVALID_ARGUMENT`. The cause was not an
incompatibility but a missing flag, and the error named it:

```python
config=types.GenerateContentConfig(
    tools=[
        types.Tool(function_declarations=[...]),
        types.Tool(google_search=types.GoogleSearch()),
    ],
    tool_config=types.ToolConfig(include_server_side_tool_invocations=True),
)
```

Consequence: the single-loop design in Approach C stands. No two-call split, no
+1h architecture change. `news_scan` and the streamed tool loop share one turn.

Second finding from the grounding check: it returned a live August 2026 figure
(Central London under offer at a 19-year high of 4.44m sq ft) that post-dates the
Q2 seed. The live layer genuinely adds information the static data does not have,
which is the monitoring story working as intended.
